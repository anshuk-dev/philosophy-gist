# Philosophy Gist: 120-Day Mastery Roadmap
**Systematic Curriculum Progression for Central University Assistant Professor & UGC NET / JRF**

---

## 🎯 Purpose & Methodology

This roadmap organizes the expansive syllabus of Indian and Western Philosophy into daily dialectical units. Each day pairs **one Indian concept** with **one Western concept**, highlighting epistemological parallels, metaphysical divergences, primary textual loci, and oral interview defense strategies.

---

## 🗓️ Phase 3: Self, Consciousness & Mind (Days 51–75)

- **Indian Focus**:
  - Nyāya's substantial Ātman (consciousness as an accidental quality / āgantuka-dharma)
  - Advaita Vedānta's Sākṣī-caitanya (witness consciousness) & Nirguṇa Brahman
  - Buddhist Anātmavāda (five skandhas, stream of consciousness / citta-santāna)
  - Jaina Jīva (organic extension, expansion and contraction according to body)
  - Kaśmīr Śaivism (Pratyabhijñā, Spanda, Śiva-Śakti non-duality)
- **Western Focus**:
  - Hume's Bundle Theory of the Self
  - Kant's Transcendental Unity of Apperception (The 'I think')
  - Sartre's Transcendence of the Ego & Bad Faith (Mauvaise Foi)
  - Merleau-Ponty's Corporeal Schema & Embodied Perception
  - Gilbert Ryle's Ghost in the Machine & Category Mistake

## 🗓️ Phase 4: Language, Logic & Contemporary Philosophy (Days 76–100)

- **Indian Focus**:
  - Bhartṛhari's Śabdādvaita and Sphoṭavāda (*Vākyapadīya*)
  - Abhihitānvayavāda (Kumārila) vs. Anvitābhidhānavāda (Prabhākara)
  - Navya-Nyāya technical language (Gangeśa: Avacchedaka, Pratiyogin, Anuyogin)
  - K.C. Bhattacharyya (*The Subject as Freedom*, *Swaraj in Ideas*)
  - Sri Aurobindo (*The Life Divine*, Supermind, Integral Yoga)
  - Swami Vivekananda (*Practical Vedānta*, Raja Yoga)
- **Western Focus**:
  - Russell's Theory of Definite Descriptions (*On Denoting*)
  - Early Wittgenstein (*Tractatus*) vs. Late Wittgenstein (*Philosophical Investigations*, Language Games)
  - Logical Positivism: Verification Principle (Ayer, Carnap)
  - Quine's *Two Dogmas of Empiricism* (Analytic-Synthetic breakdown, Web of Belief)
  - Derrida's Deconstruction & Différance; Foucault's Power/Knowledge (*Surveiller et punir*)

- **Indian Focus**:
  - Puruṣārthas (Dharma, Artha, Kāma, Mokṣa) & Vidhi-vāda

  - John Rawls' Theory of Justice (Original Position, Veil of Ignorance)
  - Robert Nozick's Entitlement Theory & Libertarianism

## 🎓 Oral Interview Strategies for Delhi University (DU) & Central Universities

1. **Never conflate sub-schools**: When asked about Mīmāṃsā, immediately clarify: *"From the Bhāṭṭa perspective or the Prābhākara perspective?"*
2. **Cite primary texts by name**: Rather than saying *"Advaita says..."*, say *"Śaṅkara states in the Adhyāsa-Bhāṣya of Brahmasūtra..."*
3. **Handle comparative questions with precision**: If asked to compare Kant and Śaṅkara, highlight that while both identify space/time/categories as subjective appearances, Śaṅkara grounds appearance in transcendental ignorance (*Avidyā*) sublatable by direct knowledge (*Aparokṣānubhūti*), whereas Kant maintains categories are inescapable conditions of finite human reason.
