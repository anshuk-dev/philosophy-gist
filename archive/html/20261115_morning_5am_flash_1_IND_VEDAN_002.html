Indian Philosophy • Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta

Rāmānuja's Aprathaksiddhi & Saptavidhā Anupapatti

Topic: Qualified Non-Dualism & Critique of Māyā
The Fundamental Dilemma
"Can non-dualism preserve the reality of individual souls, the external cosmos, and personal devotion (Bhakti) without dismissing them as illusory nescience?"
Canonical Vocabulary (Precision Lexicon)
Aprathaksiddhi (अपृथक्सिद्धि) — Inseparable relation: mode cannot exist without substance, yet is distinct from it (unlike Nyāya's Samavāya).
Śarīra-Śarīrī-Bhāva (शरीर-शरीरि-भाव) — Body-Soul analogy: the world and individual souls constitute the real body (śarīra) of God (Brahman).
Saptavidhā Anupapatti (सप्तविधा अनुपपत्ति) — Rāmānuja's 7 great incoherences of Advaita's Māyā: Āśraya, Tirodhāna, Svarūpa, Anirvacanīya, Pramāṇa, Nivartaka, Nivṛtti.
Viśiṣṭa-Aikya (विशिष्ट-ऐक्य) — Non-duality of the qualified whole; Brahman qualified by subtle souls/matter becomes Brahman qualified by gross souls/matter.
The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
Pūrvapakṣa (The Opponent's Challenge)
Śaṅkara argued that all differentiation is illusory; pure Nirguṇa Brahman alone is real, and any quality implies finitude.
Siddhānta (Philosophical Defense)
Rāmānuja in Śrībhāṣya dismantles Māyā: (1) Where does Māyā reside (Āśrayānupapatti)? Not in Brahman (pure light cannot host darkness) and not in Jīva (Jīva is produced by Māyā—circular reasoning!). (2) How can it veil self-luminous Brahman (Tirodhānānupapatti)? Veiling luminosity destroys its nature. Brahman is SAGUṆA, adorned with infinite auspicious qualities (kalyāṇa-guṇas).
Primary Textual Locus
Author: Rāmānuja • Work: Śrībhāṣya • Locus: Introduction to BS 1.1.1 (The Great Siddhānta / Mahā-siddhānta)
Selection Committee & UGC NET Trap
⚠ Interview Trap Question
Compare Aprathaksiddhi (Viśiṣṭādvaita) and Samavāya (Nyāya): Samavāya relates two ALREADY established separate substances (ayutasiddha); Aprathaksiddhi relates an attribute that CANNOT even be conceived or exist without its substantive substrate.
Wisdom Gist
"The universe is not an empty illusion to be escaped; it is the living, breathing body of the Divine, worthy of reverence, care, and loving devotion."
