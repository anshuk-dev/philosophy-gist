Pratītyasamutpāda and Nāgārjuna's Mūlamadhyamaka Śūnyatā

School: Madhyamaka Buddhism | Topic: Metaphysics: Interdependent Origination & Emptiness
1. The Core Dilemma (The Problem)
"If all phenomena arise dependently upon causes and conditions, can any entity possess independent, inherent essence (svabhāva)? Does emptiness imply nihilism, or is it the sole basis for meaningful change?"
2. Technical Lexicon & Philosophical Vocabulary
Pratītyasamutpāda (प्रतीत्यसमुत्पाद) — Dependent co-arising: nothing arises unconditionally or in isolation.
Śūnyatā (शून्यता) — Emptiness; the total absence of self-existent inherent nature (niḥsvabhāvatā) in all dharmas.
Svabhāva (स्वभाव) — Inherent, self-sufficient, immutable nature, which Madhyamaka proves to be a conceptual impossibility.
Samvṛti-satya vs. Paramārtha-satya (संवृति-सत्य / परमार्थ-सत्य) — Conventional truth (empirical pragmatism) vs. Ultimate truth (direct awareness of emptiness).
3. The Dialectic: Purvapaksha vs. Siddhanta
Pūrvapakṣa (The Opponent's Challenge)
The Sarvāstivādin Abhidharmika and Nyāya realist object: If all things are empty (śūnya), then moral action (karma), the Four Noble Truths, and liberation (nirvāṇa) become illusions. Emptiness destroys the Dhamma and leads to nihilism (ucchedavāda).
Siddhānta (Philosophical Defense & Resolution)
Nāgārjuna in MMK Chapter 24 famously turns the objection on its head: If things had Svabhāva (inherent fixed essence), they could never change, suffer, or achieve awakening! A seed could never become a sprout. It is precisely BECAUSE everything is empty of fixed essence that causal transformation, moral cultivation, and liberation are possible: 'Yah pratītyasamutpādaḥ śūnyatāṃ tāṃ pracakṣmahe'.
4. Primary Canonical Source Citation
Author: Nāgārjuna • Work: Mūlamadhyamakakārikā (MMK) • Locus: Chapter 24 (Āryasatya-parīkṣā), verses 18–19.
5. Selection Committee / University Interview Pitfall
⚠ Interview Trap Question
Interviewers frequently test if candidates equate Śūnyatā with nothingness (abhāva). Nāgārjuna explicitly warns: 'Śūnyatā is the relinquishing of all philosophical views (dṛṣṭi-sarva-prahāṇa). Those who make a dogma out of emptiness are incurable (asādhya), like a patient whom medicine itself poisons!'
6. Wisdom Gist (Reflective Synthesis)
"To be empty is not to be nothing; it is to be open to everything. When we surrender our obsession with rigid, permanent identities, we discover that life's interdependency is not a limitation, but the very ground of freedom and compassion."

Heidegger's Dasein, Being-in-the-world & Being-towards-death

School: Phenomenology / Fundamental Ontology | Topic: Ontology: The Question of Being
1. The Core Dilemma (The Problem)
"How has Western metaphysics forgotten the fundamental question of Being (Seinsvergessenheit), reducing reality to present-at-hand objects, and how does the human entity (Dasein) uncover its authentic temporal existence?"
2. Technical Lexicon & Philosophical Vocabulary
Dasein (Being-there) — The unique entity for whom its very Being is an issue; human existence characterized by disclosure and care.
In-der-Welt-sein (Being-in-the-world) — The primordial holistic engagement with equipment and context prior to subject-object separation.
Zuhandenheit (Ready-to-hand) vs. Vorhandenheit (Present-at-hand) — The practical familiarity of tools in use vs. the theoretical, detached observation of bare objects.
Sein-zum-Tode (Being-towards-death) — The authentic confrontation with one's non-relational, unavoidable finitude that shatters everyday conformism (das Man).
3. The Dialectic: Purvapaksha vs. Siddhanta
Pūrvapakṣa (The Opponent's Challenge)
The Cartesian tradition posited a detached thinking subject (res cogitans) observing an external spatial reality of inert physical substances (res extensa).
Siddhānta (Philosophical Defense & Resolution)
Heidegger demonstrates in Being and Time (1927) that we do not encounter the world primarily through detached theoretical contemplation. Our primordial mode of Being is practical absorption (care / Sorge) in a network of significance (the workshop hammer). Detached Cartesian subject-object dualism is a derivative, impoverished abstraction that occurs only when the hammer breaks.
4. Primary Canonical Source Citation
Author: Martin Heidegger • Work: Being and Time (Sein und Zeit) • Locus: Division I (§§ 14–18 on Worldhood and Readiness-to-hand) and Division II (§§ 46–53 on Death and Authenticity).
5. Selection Committee / University Interview Pitfall
⚠ Interview Trap Question
Distinguish Husserl's phenomenology from Heidegger's: Husserl seeks a transcendental, presuppositionless consciousness via the Epoché (bracketing the world); Heidegger argues that consciousness cannot step outside Being-in-the-world; Dasein is always already 'thrown' (Geworfenheit) into a historical, linguistic context (hermeneutic phenomenology).
6. Wisdom Gist (Reflective Synthesis)
"We do not reside inside our minds peering out through glass windows at a foreign universe. We are fundamentally woven into the world. To live authentically is to own our finitude and make conscious choices before time runs out."

Central University Interview & UGC NET Practice MCQs

  • (A) Madhyamaka Buddhism
  • (B) Carvaka
  • (C) Nyaya
  • (D) Samkhya
Correct: (A) — This is the cornerstone thesis of Madhyamaka Buddhism.
Q2. In Being and Time (Sein und Zeit), the primary argument focuses on:
  • (A) Heidegger's Dasein, Being-in-the-world & Being-towards-death
  • (B) Skepticism alone
  • (C) Dualism alone
  • (D) Pure materialism
Correct: (A) — Argued directly in Being and Time (Sein und Zeit) by Martin Heidegger.
