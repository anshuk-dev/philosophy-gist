Indian Philosophy • Nyāya

Hetvābhāsa: The Five Inferential Fallacies

Topic: Fallacies of Inference
The Fundamental Dilemma
"What precise logical defects destroy the validity of an inference, causing the reason (hetu) to yield error or contradiction?"
Canonical Vocabulary (Precision Lexicon)
Savyabhicāra / Anaikāntika (सव्यभिचार) — The erratic/inconclusive reason: Sādhāraṇa (too wide), Asādhāraṇa (too narrow), Anupasaṃhārī (non-exclusive).
Viruddha (विरुद्ध) — The contradictory reason: establishes the exact opposite of what was to be proved (e.g., 'Sound is eternal because it is produced').
Satpratipakṣa (सत्प्रतिपक्ष) — The counterbalanced reason: an equally strong contrary inference exists against it.
Asiddha / Sādhyasama (असिद्ध) — The unproved/unfounded reason: Āśrayāsiddha (unreal subject), Svarūpāsiddha (absent in subject), Vyāpyatvāsiddha (conditional).
Bādhita / Kālātīta (बाधित) — The sublated reason: the conclusion is directly contradicted by a stronger pramāṇa like perception ('Fire is cold because it is a substance').
The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
Pūrvapakṣa (The Opponent's Challenge)
Early thinkers wondered: Why are there 5 distinct fallacies rather than simply one overarching category of 'invalid inference'?
Siddhānta (Philosophical Defense)
Nyāya systematically deduces the 5 fallacies from the 5 essential characteristics of a sound reason (rūpa-pañcaka): (1) presence in pakṣa, (2) presence in sapakṣa, (3) absence in vipakṣa, (4) absence of counterbalancing reason, (5) non-sublation by perception. Each fallacy corresponds to the breakdown of one specific leg.
Primary Textual Locus
Author: Annambhaṭṭa & Keśava Miśra • Work: Tarkasaṃgraha & Tarkabhāṣā • Locus: Tarkasaṃgraha §§ 52–58; Tarkabhāṣā on Hetvābhāsa
Selection Committee & UGC NET Trap
⚠ Interview Trap Question
What is the crucial difference between Satpratipakṣa and Bādhita? In Satpratipakṣa, the opposition is from another INFERENCE of equal strength; in Bādhita, the conclusion is cancelled by a non-inferential, immediate source like PERCEPTION (e.g., touch tells us fire is hot).
Wisdom Gist
"A false reason does not merely fail to prove truth; it seduces the mind into dogmatism. Rigorous thinking begins by interrogating the unstated flaws in our own arguments before attacking others."
