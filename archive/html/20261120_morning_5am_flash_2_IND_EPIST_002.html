Indian Philosophy • Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika

Pratyakṣa: Nirvikalpaka vs. Savikalpaka & Ṣaḍvidha-Sannikarṣa

Topic: Perception & Sense Contact
The Fundamental Dilemma
"How does sensory contact with an external object translate from a raw sensory datum into a structured, conceptual judgment ('This is a brown earthen pot')?"
Canonical Vocabulary (Precision Lexicon)
Nirvikalpaka Pratyakṣa (निर्विकल्पक प्रत्यक्ष) — Indeterminate, non-propositional perception apprehending bare structure without name or genus.
Savikalpaka Pratyakṣa (सविकल्पक प्रत्यक्ष) — Determinate, propositional perception grasping the object qualified by name, genus, and attribute (viśeṣya-viśeṣaṇa-sambandha).
Ṣaḍvidha-Sannikarṣa (षड्विध सन्निकर्ष) — The sixfold sense-object contacts: Saṃyoga, Saṃyukta-samavāya, Saṃyukta-samaveta-samavāya, Samavāya, Samaveta-samavāya, Viśeṣaṇa-viśeṣya-bhāva.
Alaukika Pratyakṣa (अलौकिक प्रत्यक्ष) — Extraordinary perception: Sāmānyalakṣaṇa (genus), Jñānalakṣaṇa (complication/memory), Yogaja (intuitive gnosis).
The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
Pūrvapakṣa (The Opponent's Challenge)
The Buddhist epistemologist (Dharmakīrti) argues: Perception is strictly non-conceptual (kalpanāpoḍham). The moment language, name, or universal is applied, it is no longer perception but mental construction (vikalpa). Savikalpaka is not perception at all.
Siddhānta (Philosophical Defense)
The Naiyāyika retorts: The universal (sāmānya) is a real objective entity inhering in the object. Savikalpaka perception directly sees the real substance as qualified by its real universal; words merely express what the sense faculty has already veridically grasped.
Primary Textual Locus
Author: Gautama & Annambhaṭṭa • Work: Nyāyasūtra & Tarkasaṃgraha • Locus: Nyāyasūtra 1.1.4 ('indriyārthasannikarṣotpannaṃ jñānam...'); Tarkasaṃgraha Dīpikā §30
Selection Committee & UGC NET Trap
⚠ Interview Trap Question
How does Nyāya perceive absence (abhāva)? Through the 6th sannikarṣa: 'Viśeṣaṇa-viśeṣya-bhāva' (the absence of pot is an adjective qualifying the bare ground). Contrast this with Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā, which invents a separate 6th pramāṇa: Anupalabdhi (non-apprehension).
Wisdom Gist
"Human perception is never merely passive recording; our cognitive faculties instantly weave raw immediacy into an intelligible, structured reality of meaning."
