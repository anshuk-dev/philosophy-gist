Aristotle: The Four Causes, Hylomorphism & Actuality/Potentiality

Topic: Substance, Hylomorphism & Causes
The Fundamental Dilemma
"How does natural change occur without creating reality out of nothing or freezing reality into Parmenidean immobility?"
Canonical Vocabulary (Precision Lexicon)
Hylomorphism — Every concrete substance (ousia) is an indissoluble composite of Matter (potency) and Form (actuality).
Four Causes (Aitia) — Material (bronze), Formal (statue shape), Efficient (sculptor), Final (telos/honor).
Dunamis vs. Energeia — The acorn is potentially an oak tree; the mature oak tree is the realized actuality.
Telos (Teleology) — Nature does nothing in vain; every entity is pulled forward by its inherent purpose or natural goal.
The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
Pūrvapakṣa (The Opponent's Challenge)
Plato separated the Form from the concrete horse, creating an unbridgeable metaphysical chasm.
Siddhānta (Philosophical Defense)
Aristotle in Metaphysics Book VII argues: If forms exist separately, how do they cause change? Forms exist ONLY when instantiated in matter (except for the Unmoved Mover). Change is not impossible; change is the actualization of potentiality (energeia of dunamis).
Primary Textual Locus
Author: Aristotle • Work: Physics & Metaphysics • Locus: Physics II.3 (Four Causes); Metaphysics VII–IX (Substance and Actuality)
Selection Committee & UGC NET Trap
⚠ Interview Trap Question
What is 'Primary Substance' (Prōtē Ousia) for Aristotle in the Categories vs Metaphysics? In the Categories, primary substance is the individual concrete particular (this man, this horse); in Metaphysics VII, it is identified with the essential Form that makes the matter this specific thing.
