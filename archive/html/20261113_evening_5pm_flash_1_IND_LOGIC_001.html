Indian Philosophy • Nyāya

Anumāna: The Pañcāvayava Syllogism & Vyāpti

Topic: Inference & Epistemic Induction
The Fundamental Dilemma
"How can the mind legitimately cross the gap between what is currently perceived (smoke on a mountain) and what is hidden from view (fire inside the mountain)?"
Canonical Vocabulary (Precision Lexicon)
Vyāpti (व्याप्ति) — Invariable, unconditional relation of concomitance between the sign (hetu/smoke) and the signified (sādhya/fire).
Pañcāvayava (पञ्चावयव) — The 5 members of demonstration: Pratijñā (thesis), Hetu (reason), Udāharaṇa (universal exemplification), Upanaya (application), Nigamana (conclusion).
Upādhi (उपाधि) — An extraneous condition that renders a concomitance conditional (e.g., 'wet fuel' is the upādhi for smoke from fire).
Parāmarśa (परामर्श) — The synthetic cognitive act: realization that the mountain possesses smoke which is invariably accompanied by fire.
The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
Pūrvapakṣa (The Opponent's Challenge)
Cārvāka challenges: How do you know Vyāpti holds universally? You have seen fire with smoke in a kitchen, but have you seen every fire in all past, present, and future universes? If you infer it, you presuppose inference; induction is logically circular.
Siddhānta (Philosophical Defense)
Nyāya solves the Indian problem of induction through 'Sāmānyalakṣaṇa-pratyakṣa' (perceiving smokiness as a universal in all smoke) combined with 'Tarka' (reductio ad absurdum: 'If smoke could occur without fire, why does no one look for smoke in a lake?').
Primary Textual Locus
Author: Vātsyāyana & Uddyotakāra • Work: Nyāyabhāṣya & Nyāyavārttika • Locus: Nyāyasūtra 1.1.5; Tarkasaṃgraha §§ 36–48
Selection Committee & UGC NET Trap
⚠ Interview Trap Question
Compare the Nyāya 5-membered syllogism with Aristotle's 3-membered syllogism: Aristotle's syllogism is purely deductive and formal; the Nyāya pañcāvayava is BOTH inductive and deductive, requiring an empirical example (Udāharaṇa) from the world.
Wisdom Gist
"Inference is the bridge between the finite senses and the infinite world. Reason does not float in formal abstraction; it remains anchored to lived empirical observation."
