Swami Vivekananda: Practical Vedānta & Universal Religion

Topic: Practical Vedānta & Social Service
The Fundamental Dilemma
"How can the supreme philosophical truth of Advaita (oneness of Brahman) be translated into social action, moral courage, and global religious harmony?"
Canonical Vocabulary (Precision Lexicon)
Practical Vedānta — The application of non-dual spiritual realization to everyday social, educational, and ethical life.
Daridra Nārāyaṇa (God in the Poor) — Serving the suffering and downtrodden as direct worship of the living Divine.
Universal Religion — Harmony of all faiths as different paths ascending the same spiritual mountain, avoiding dogmatism.
Four Yogas (Jñāna, Bhakti, Karma, Rāja) — A balanced, scientific synthesis of intellect, devotion, action, and psychological meditation.
The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
Pūrvapakṣa (The Opponent's Challenge)
Critics claimed Vedānta fosters quietism, fatalism, and social passivity because it treats the world as an illusion.
Siddhānta (Philosophical Defense)
Vivekananda roared at the Parliament of Religions (1893) and in his London lectures: 'If you cannot worship your brother man, the manifested God, how can you worship an unmanifested God?' If all souls are Brahman, all human beings are inherently divine and equal. Service is not condescending charity; it is self-worship.
Primary Textual Locus
Author: Swami Vivekananda • Work: Complete Works of Swami Vivekananda • Locus: Volume II: 'Practical Vedānta' (Lectures delivered in London, November 1896)
Selection Committee & UGC NET Trap
⚠ Interview Trap Question
Central University boards frequently ask: 'How does Vivekananda's concept of Maya differ from Shankara's?' Vivekananda did NOT treat Maya as an ontological illusion or theory; he called Maya 'a simple statement of fact of what we are and what we see around us'—the inescapable contradiction between the ideal and the real.
Wisdom Gist
"Strength is life; weakness is death. True spirituality is not running away from the world, but bringing the light of the divine into the dark corners of human suffering."
