## Madhvācārya: Pañca-Bheda & Svatantra-Asvatantra Tattva (Indian Philosophy)
**School:** Dvaita Vedānta | **Unit:** UNIT_01

### 🔗 Conceptual Chain (Dialectical Lineage)
- **1. Origin / Prior Knot:** Śaṅkara's Non-Dualism & Rāmānuja's Qualified Non-Dualism
- **2. Current Core Pivot:** **Reality is governed by five absolute, eternal, unbridgeable differences (Pañca-Bheda); God alone is independent.**
- **3. Next Horizon:** Bhakti & Eternal Predestination (Tāratamya)

### 🎯 The Fundamental Dilemma
> "Can scriptural assertions of difference between the finite soul and the infinite God be taken literally without being dissolved into non-dualism?"

### 🏛️ Canonical Technical Lexicon
- **Pañca-Bheda (पञ्च-भेद)**: Five eternal differences: God-Soul, God-Matter, Soul-Soul, Soul-Matter, Matter-Matter.
- **Svatantra vs. Asvatantra**: The sole Independent Reality (Viṣṇu) vs Dependent Realities (souls and world).
- **Viśeṣa (विशेष)**: Internal differentiating power that accounts for distinction without multiplying substances.
- **Bimba-Pratibimba-Bhāva**: Soul is an eternal reflection of God, distinct yet totally dependent.

### ⚔️ The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
**Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):** Advaita claims 'Tat Tvam Asi' (Thou Art That) teaches non-dual identity between Jīva and Brahman.

**Siddhānta (Defense):** Madhva reads the Chāndogya text as 'Atat Tvam Asi' (Thou art NOT that!) or interprets it as similarity in essence. Perception and scripture everywhere confirm difference. Difference (Bheda) is the constitutional nature of reality (svarūpa-bheda).

### 📜 Primary Textual Locus
- **Author:** Madhvācārya
- **Work:** *Viṣṇutattvanirṇaya*
- **Passage:** Section on the five eternal differences (Pañca-bheda)

### ⚠️ Central University Interview & UGC NET Trap
> How does Madhva explain the relation between God and His qualities without multiplying entities? Madhva introduces 'Viśeṣa'—an internal differentiator that functions in place of difference without being an independent entity.

### 💡 Wisdom Gist
> "Acknowledging our finitude and honoring the reality of the other is not spiritual defeat; it is the humble foundation of reverence, gratitude, and devotion."
