## Anekāntavāda, Syādvāda & Nayavāda (Indian Philosophy)
**School:** Jainism | **Unit:** UNIT_01

### 🔗 Conceptual Chain (Dialectical Lineage)
- **1. Origin / Prior Knot:** Dogmatic absolutisms (Advaita's eternal permanence vs Buddhism's flux)
- **2. Current Core Pivot:** **Reality is multifaceted and complex (Anekānta); every predication must be conditional and standpoint-relative (Syādvāda).**
- **3. Next Horizon:** Jaina Ethics: Ahiṃsā as the intellectual duty of respecting rival philosophical perspectives

### 🎯 The Fundamental Dilemma
> "How can philosophy overcome the dogmatic clashes between systems that assert reality is purely permanent (Vedānta) and systems that assert it is purely momentary (Buddhism)?"

### 🏛️ Canonical Technical Lexicon
- **Anekāntavāda (अनेकान्तवाद)**: Metaphysical doctrine of non-one-sidedness: reality possesses infinite attributes (ananta-dharmātmakam vastu).
- **Syādvāda (स्याद्वाद)**: Epistemological doctrine of conditional predication: using 'Syāt' (in some respect) to avoid dogmatism.
- **Saptabhaṅgī (सप्तभङ्गी)**: Seven-fold predication: syād asti, syān nāsti, syād asti nāsti ca, syād avaktavyaḥ, etc.
- **Nayavāda (नयवाद)**: Theory of partial standpoints: examining reality through specialized perspectives (e.g., Dravyārthika vs Paryāyārthika naya).

### ⚔️ The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
**Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):** Śaṅkara and the Naiyāyikas attack: Syādvāda is sheer nonsense. A thing cannot be both hot and cold at the same time; qualifying everything with 'somehow' violates the law of contradiction.

**Siddhānta (Defense):** The Jaina answers: It is not in the SAME respect that a thing is and is not! A gold ring IS from the standpoint of substance (gold), but IS NOT from the standpoint of previous form (necklace). Absolute permanent vs absolute momentary are one-sided abstractions (ekāntas). Reality is Dravya-Guṇa-Paryāya (substance with enduring qualities undergoing modes).

### 📜 Primary Textual Locus
- **Author:** Mallisena & Kundakunda
- **Work:** *Syādvādamañjarī & Pravacanasāra*
- **Passage:** Syādvādamañjarī on Kārikā 28; Pravacanasāra Book II

### ⚠️ Central University Interview & UGC NET Trap
> Understand why 'Avaktavya' (inexpressible) is the 4th step in Saptabhaṅgī: It occurs when one attempts to state existence and non-existence SIMULTANEOUSLY (yugapat), which language cannot do. Contrast with the 3rd step which states them sequentially (kramaśaḥ).

### 💡 Wisdom Gist
> "Intellectual humility is the highest form of non-violence. Recognizing that our personal viewpoint captures only one facet of the infinite jewel of reality disarms dogmatism."
