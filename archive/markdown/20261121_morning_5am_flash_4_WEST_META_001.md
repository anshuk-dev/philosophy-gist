## Hume's Problem of Induction & The Critique of Necessary Connection (Western Philosophy)
**School:** British Empiricism / Skepticism | **Unit:** UNIT_03

### 🔗 Conceptual Chain (Dialectical Lineage)
- **1. Origin / Prior Knot:** Rationalist Causal Metaphysics & Empiricist Association
- **2. Current Core Pivot:** **Causation has no observable necessity; induction is grounded not in reason, but in psychological custom (habit).**
- **3. Next Horizon:** Kant's Awakening from Dogmatic Slumber (The Synthetic A Priori)

### 🎯 The Fundamental Dilemma
> "Can we logically demonstrate that the future will resemble the past, or that causes contain a necessary power to produce their effects?"

### 🏛️ Canonical Technical Lexicon
- **Hume's Fork**: All inquiries divided into Relations of Ideas (analytic/a priori) and Matters of Fact (synthetic/a posteriori).
- **Constant Conjunction**: Repeated observation of event B following event A in time and space, without any visible tie of necessity.
- **Custom or Habit**: The psychological instinct that leads animals and humans to expect like effects from like causes.
- **Uniformity of Nature**: The inductive presupposition that nature behaves uniformly, which cannot be proved without circularity.

### ⚔️ The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
**Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):** Descartes and Spinoza asserted that causes necessarily contain their effects with mathematical certainty discoverable by intellect.

**Siddhānta (Defense):** Hume demonstrates in Section IV of the Enquiry: Inspect a billiard ball striking another. You see movement, contact, and the second ball rolling. You NEVER perceive the 'power' or 'necessary connection'. Attempting to prove induction by induction is circular reasoning (begging the question).

### 📜 Primary Textual Locus
- **Author:** David Hume
- **Work:** *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*
- **Passage:** Sections IV & VII

### ⚠️ Central University Interview & UGC NET Trap
> Selection committees love to ask: 'Did Hume advise abandoning science and causal expectations?' Hume answered NO! Skeptical doubt shows the limits of theoretical reason, but practical life must follow natural instinct: 'Be a philosopher; but, amidst all your philosophy, be still a man.'

### 💡 Wisdom Gist
> "Intellectual pride claims universal certitude, but philosophical wisdom recognizes that our deepest expectations rest on the humble habits of nature."
