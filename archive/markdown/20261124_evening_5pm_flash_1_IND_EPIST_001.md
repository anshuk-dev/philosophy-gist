## Svataḥ Prāmāṇyavāda vs. Parataḥ Prāmāṇyavāda (Indian Philosophy)
**School:** Mīmāṃsā / Nyāya | **Unit:** UNIT_01

### 🔗 Conceptual Chain (Dialectical Lineage)
- **1. Origin / Prior Knot:** Cārvāka Skepticism (Doubt of all unperceived entities)
- **2. Current Core Pivot:** **Cognition carries its own truth at inception (Svataḥ), or requires external pragmatic verification (Parataḥ).**
- **3. Next Horizon:** Theories of Illusion (If knowledge is self-valid, how does error occur? -> Khyātivāda)

### 🎯 The Fundamental Dilemma
> "Does a valid cognition (pramā) possess intrinsic validity from the very moment of its emergence, or must truth be proven by an external test?"

### 🏛️ Canonical Technical Lexicon
- **Svataḥ Prāmāṇya (स्वतः प्रामाण्य)**: Intrinsic validity; self-authentication of cognition in origin (utpattau) and apprehension (jñāptau).
- **Parataḥ Prāmāṇya (परतः प्रामाण्य)**: Extrinsic validity; verified via causal excellence (guṇa) and successful action (pravṛtti-sāmarthya).
- **Anavasthā (अनवस्था)**: Infinite regress that occurs if every validating cognition itself needs validation.
- **Bādhaka-jñāna (बाधकज्ञान)**: Sublating cognition that cancels an erroneous perception.

### ⚔️ The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
**Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):** The Nyāya argues: If all cognitions were intrinsically valid, doubts and illusions would never happen. A thirsty traveler sees a mirage; only reaching the pool and drinking (pravṛtti-sāmarthya) verifies the cognition as true.

**Siddhānta (Defense):** Kumārila Bhaṭṭa counters: If cognition A needs cognition B to validate it, and B needs C, knowledge never arrives at certainty (anavasthā). Cognition is born valid from its own apparatus; falsity alone is extrinsic, exposed by a defeater (bādhaka).

### 📜 Primary Textual Locus
- **Author:** Kumārila Bhaṭṭa
- **Work:** *Ślokavārttika*
- **Passage:** Codanā-sūtra adhikaraṇa, verses 33–61

### ⚠️ Central University Interview & UGC NET Trap
> Distinguish Bhāṭṭa from Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā: Bhāṭṭas hold that cognition is supersensuous and inferred from 'knownness' (jñātatā) in the object, whereas Prābhākaras reject jñātatā, argue for 'Tripuṭī-pratyakṣa' (simultaneous awareness of knower, known, knowing), and claim NO cognition is ever false (Akhyātivāda).

### 💡 Wisdom Gist
> "At the root of thought lies a baseline epistemic trust. Without trusting our primary apprehension until contradicted, the human mind falls into paralyzing infinite regression."
