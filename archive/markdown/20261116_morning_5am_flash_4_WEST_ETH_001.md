## Kant: The Categorical Imperative & Kingdom of Ends (Western Philosophy)
**School:** Kantian Moral Philosophy | **Unit:** UNIT_04

### 🔗 Conceptual Chain (Dialectical Lineage)
- **1. Origin / Prior Knot:** Utilitarianism & Moral Sentimentalism (Hume: Reason is slave of passions)
- **2. Current Core Pivot:** **Morality is grounded in pure practical reason; act only on maxims that can be willed as universal laws, treating persons as ends.**
- **3. Next Horizon:** Hegel's Critique of Kantian Moral Rigorism (Sittlichkeit vs Moralität)

### 🎯 The Fundamental Dilemma
> "Can moral duty hold unconditional necessity if moral rules depend on human desires, consequences, or emotional inclinations?"

### 🏛️ Canonical Technical Lexicon
- **Categorical vs. Hypothetical Imperative**: Unconditional duty ('Do X!') vs Conditional prudence ('If you want Y, do X').
- **Formula of Universal Law**: Act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law.
- **Formula of Humanity**: Act in such a way that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or another, always as an end and never merely as a means.
- **Autonomy of the Will**: The moral agent is both author and subject of the moral law, free from heteronomous desires.

### ⚔️ The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
**Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):** Utilitarians argue: An act is good because of its beneficial consequences; lying to save an innocent life produces net happiness.

**Siddhānta (Defense):** Kant insists in the Groundwork: Only a GOOD WILL is good without qualification. If everyone made lying promises when in trouble, the very institution of promising would logically self-destruct (contradiction in conception). Moral worth lies in duty for duty's sake, not consequences.

### 📜 Primary Textual Locus
- **Author:** Immanuel Kant
- **Work:** *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals*
- **Passage:** Sections I & II (Akademie 4:393–440)

### ⚠️ Central University Interview & UGC NET Trap
> Distinguish 'Perfect Duties' from 'Imperfect Duties' in Kant: Perfect duties (do not lie, do not commit suicide) result in a 'contradiction in CONCEPTION' if universalized; Imperfect duties (cultivate talents, help others) result in a 'contradiction in the WILL'.

### 💡 Wisdom Gist
> "Human beings possess dignity, not a price. To treat another human being as a mere tool for our own convenience is to betray the sacred light of reason within ourselves."
