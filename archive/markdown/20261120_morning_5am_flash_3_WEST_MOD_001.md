## Descartes' Method of Doubt & The Cogito (Western Philosophy)
**School:** Continental Rationalism | **Unit:** UNIT_03

### 🔗 Conceptual Chain (Dialectical Lineage)
- **1. Origin / Prior Knot:** Medieval Scholastic Dogmatism & Montaigne's Skepticism
- **2. Current Core Pivot:** **By pushing doubt to its hyper-radical limit (evil demon), the indubitable bedrock of the thinking self (Cogito) is discovered.**
- **3. Next Horizon:** Cartesian Dualism & The Mind-Body Interaction Problem (Spinoza's Monism)

### 🎯 The Fundamental Dilemma
> "How can human cognition discover an indubitable, rock-solid foundation for knowledge that withstands all skeptical assault?"

### 🏛️ Canonical Technical Lexicon
- **Methodological Doubt (Doubt as an instrument)**: Systematic suspension of assent to any belief that admits of the slightest uncertainty.
- **Malin Génie (Evil Demon)**: The ultimate skeptical thought experiment: an all-powerful deceiver manipulating math and sensory perceptions.
- **Cogito, Ergo Sum**: I think, therefore I am; the self-authenticating certainty that doubt requires a doubter.
- **Res Cogitans vs. Res Extensa**: Substance dualism: thinking unextended substance (mind) vs unthinking extended substance (matter).

### ⚔️ The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
**Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):** The radical skeptic asserts: We cannot trust our senses (dreams deceive us) and we cannot trust mathematics (a malicious demon could deceive us every time we add 2 and 3). Knowledge is impossible.

**Siddhānta (Defense):** Descartes points out in Meditation II: Let the demon deceive me as much as he can, he can NEVER bring it about that I am nothing so long as I think that I am something. Even to be deceived, I must exist. Cogito is self-verifying.

### 📜 Primary Textual Locus
- **Author:** René Descartes
- **Work:** *Meditations on First Philosophy*
- **Passage:** Meditation I & Meditation II (AT VII, 24–34)

### ⚠️ Central University Interview & UGC NET Trap
> Did Descartes mean 'I think, therefore I am' as a syllogistic inference with a hidden major premise ('Whatever thinks exists')? NO! Descartes clarifies in the Second Replies that the Cogito is a direct, immediate intellectual intuition, not a syllogism.

### 💡 Wisdom Gist
> "Even in the deepest crisis of doubt, consciousness discovers its own indomitable presence. The capacity to question is proof of our being."
