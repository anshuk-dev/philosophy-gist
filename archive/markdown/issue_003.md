## Flashcard 1 (Indian Philosophy)
### The Five Classic Khyātivādas (Theory of Illusion)
**School:** Comparative Indian Epistemology | **Unit:** UNIT_06

**The Problem:**
> When a person mistakes a rope for a snake or mother-of-pearl (śukti) for silver (rajata), what is the ontological and cognitive status of the erroneous object perceived?

**Key Lexicon:**
- **Anirvacanīyakhyāti (अनिर्वचनीयख्याति)**: Advaita Vedānta: The illusory silver is indescribable as either real (sat) or unreal (asat); it is produced by Māyā/Avidyā and cancelled by true cognition.
- **Anyathākhyāti (अन्यथाख्याति)**: Nyāya: The object is real, but perceived 'otherwise' (anyathā) due to memory of real silver seen elsewhere revivified by extraordinary sense-contact (jñānalakṣaṇa-sannikarṣa).
- **Akhyāti (अख्याति)**: Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā: Non-apprehension; error is not a single misperception but failure to distinguish between direct perception of shell and memory of silver.
- **Ātmakhyāti (आत्मख्याति)**: Yogācāra Buddhism: The illusory object is an internal mental idea projected outwards as external.
- **Asatkhyāti (असत्ख्याति)**: Madhyamaka / Cārvāka: The illusory object is utterly non-existent (asat) being perceived as existent.

**The Dialectic:**
- *Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):* The Naiyāyika attacks Advaita: How can an object be 'indescribable'? An entity must be either real or unreal; the law of excluded middle demands it. The silver is real silver existing in a market stall, presented here through defect and memory.
- *Siddhānta (Resolution):* Śaṅkara and Madhusūdana Sarasvatī counter: The silver cannot be SAT (real), because it is sublated (bādhita) when the shell is seen. It cannot be ASAT (utterly non-existent like a hare's horn or sky-flower), because it was directly experienced and caused fear or greed. Hence, error forces us to recognize an ontological category beyond binary reality: Anirvacanīya (ontologically indescribable).

**Primary Text:** Śaṅkarācārya, *Brahmasūtra-Bhāṣya* (Adhyāsa-Bhāṣya (Preamble on Superimposition): 'Smṛtirūpaḥ paratra pūrvadṛṣṭāvabhāsaḥ'.)

**⚠️ University Interview Trap:**
A favorite UGC NET / University interview question: 'Which school accepts Viparyaya-khyāti?' (Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā, which is a variant of Anyathākhyāti). Another trap: Prābhākaras do NOT accept that error is an active positive misjudgment; they call it 'Vivekāgraha' (non-discrimination of two real valid cognitions).

**Wisdom Gist:**
> "Error is not mere nothingness; it has an undeniable phenomenological presence that shapes actions and emotions until the moment of sublation. Liberation begins when we realize our most vivid convictions may be glittering shells mistaken for silver."

## Flashcard 2 (Western Philosophy)
### Frege's Sense & Reference vs. Russell's Theory of Definite Descriptions
**School:** Analytic Philosophy | **Unit:** UNIT_04

**The Problem:**
> How can statements of identity between distinct terms (e.g., 'The Morning Star is the Evening Star') be informative, and how can statements about non-existent entities (e.g., 'The present King of France is bald') be meaningful and truth-evaluable?

**Key Lexicon:**
- **Sinn (Sense)**: Frege: The 'mode of presentation' (Art des Gegebenseins) of an object contained within a linguistic expression.
- **Bedeutung (Reference / Meaning)**: Frege: The actual worldly object or truth-value designated by the expression.
- **Definite Description**: Russell: Expressions of the form 'the so-and-so' (e.g., 'the author of Waverley') which appear to be singular terms but are grammatically disguised existential quantifiers.
- **Primary vs. Secondary Occurrence**: Russell's scope distinction resolving ambiguities in modal and intensional contexts regarding non-referring descriptions.

**The Dialectic:**
- *Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):* Alexius Meinong argued that since 'The present King of France' is the subject of meaningful discourse, it must possess some form of being ('Aussersein' or subsistence), even if it lacks empirical existence.
- *Siddhānta (Resolution):* Russell in 'On Denoting' (1905) rejected Meinong's ontological zoo by showing that surface grammar deceives us. 'The King of France is bald' does not refer to an unreal king; its logical form is a conjunction of three claims: (1) There exists an x such that x is King of France; (2) For all y, if y is King of France, y = x (uniqueness); (3) x is bald. Because premise 1 is false, the whole sentence is simply FALSE, with no need for subsisting entities.

**Primary Text:** Bertrand Russell & Gottlob Frege, *Russell's 'On Denoting' (Mind, 1905) & Frege's 'Über Sinn und Bedeutung' (1892)* (Russell, Mind 14 (1905): 479–493; Frege, Zeitschrift für Philosophie und philosophische Kritik 100 (1892): 25–50.)

**⚠️ University Interview Trap:**
Compare Frege and Russell on truth-values of sentences with non-referring singular terms: For Frege, 'The King of France is bald' has a Sense (Sinn) but lacks a Reference (Bedeutung); thus it has a TRUTH-VALUE GAP (neither true nor false). For Russell, the same sentence is strictly FALSE.

**Wisdom Gist:**
> "Language is a master of illusion. Many of the deepest metaphysical phantoms that have tortured human minds for millennia evaporate the moment we distinguish the superficial grammar of words from the rigorous logical architecture of thought."

## Central University & UGC NET Practice Questions

**Q1. Which school propounds the doctrine of The Five Classic Khyātivādas (Theory of Illusion)?**
- (A) Comparative Indian Epistemology
- (B) Carvaka
- (C) Nyaya
- (D) Samkhya
**Answer:** (A) &mdash; *This is the cornerstone thesis of Comparative Indian Epistemology.*

**Q2. In Russell's 'On Denoting' (Mind, 1905) & Frege's 'Über Sinn und Bedeutung' (1892), the primary argument focuses on:**
- (A) Frege's Sense & Reference vs. Russell's Theory of Definite Descriptions
- (B) Skepticism alone
- (C) Dualism alone
- (D) Pure materialism
**Answer:** (A) &mdash; *Argued directly in Russell's 'On Denoting' (Mind, 1905) & Frege's 'Über Sinn und Bedeutung' (1892) by Bertrand Russell & Gottlob Frege.*
