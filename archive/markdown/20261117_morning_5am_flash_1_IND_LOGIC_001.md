## Anumāna: The Pañcāvayava Syllogism & Vyāpti (Indian Philosophy)
**School:** Nyāya | **Unit:** UNIT_01

### 🔗 Conceptual Chain (Dialectical Lineage)
- **1. Origin / Prior Knot:** Pratyakṣa (Perception is limited to present objects)
- **2. Current Core Pivot:** **Inference extends knowledge to unperceived realms through an unbroken invariant relation (Vyāpti) formulated in a five-membered syllogism.**
- **3. Next Horizon:** Hetvābhāsa (The fallacies that rupture Vyāpti and corrupt inference)

### 🎯 The Fundamental Dilemma
> "How can the mind legitimately cross the gap between what is currently perceived (smoke on a mountain) and what is hidden from view (fire inside the mountain)?"

### 🏛️ Canonical Technical Lexicon
- **Vyāpti (व्याप्ति)**: Invariable, unconditional relation of concomitance between the sign (hetu/smoke) and the signified (sādhya/fire).
- **Pañcāvayava (पञ्चावयव)**: The 5 members of demonstration: Pratijñā (thesis), Hetu (reason), Udāharaṇa (universal exemplification), Upanaya (application), Nigamana (conclusion).
- **Upādhi (उपाधि)**: An extraneous condition that renders a concomitance conditional (e.g., 'wet fuel' is the upādhi for smoke from fire).
- **Parāmarśa (परामर्श)**: The synthetic cognitive act: realization that the mountain possesses smoke which is invariably accompanied by fire.

### ⚔️ The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
**Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):** Cārvāka challenges: How do you know Vyāpti holds universally? You have seen fire with smoke in a kitchen, but have you seen every fire in all past, present, and future universes? If you infer it, you presuppose inference; induction is logically circular.

**Siddhānta (Defense):** Nyāya solves the Indian problem of induction through 'Sāmānyalakṣaṇa-pratyakṣa' (perceiving smokiness as a universal in all smoke) combined with 'Tarka' (reductio ad absurdum: 'If smoke could occur without fire, why does no one look for smoke in a lake?').

### 📜 Primary Textual Locus
- **Author:** Vātsyāyana & Uddyotakāra
- **Work:** *Nyāyabhāṣya & Nyāyavārttika*
- **Passage:** Nyāyasūtra 1.1.5; Tarkasaṃgraha §§ 36–48

### ⚠️ Central University Interview & UGC NET Trap
> Compare the Nyāya 5-membered syllogism with Aristotle's 3-membered syllogism: Aristotle's syllogism is purely deductive and formal; the Nyāya pañcāvayava is BOTH inductive and deductive, requiring an empirical example (Udāharaṇa) from the world.

### 💡 Wisdom Gist
> "Inference is the bridge between the finite senses and the infinite world. Reason does not float in formal abstraction; it remains anchored to lived empirical observation."
