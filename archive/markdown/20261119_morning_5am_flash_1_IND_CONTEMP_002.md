## Dr. B. R. Ambedkar: Navayāna & Annihilation of Caste (Indian Philosophy)
**School:** Contemporary Indian / Social-Political | **Unit:** UNIT_05

### 🔗 Conceptual Chain (Dialectical Lineage)
- **1. Origin / Prior Knot:** Orthodox Brahmanical Caste Sanctions & Hierarchical Dharma
- **2. Current Core Pivot:** **Religion must be reconstructed on universal human dignity, liberty, equality, and fraternity; caste is a graded inequality that destroys fraternity.**
- **3. Next Horizon:** Constitutional Morality & Secular Democracy in Modern India

### 🎯 The Fundamental Dilemma
> "Can a religion remain valid if it sanctifies hereditary, graded inequality, and how does the rational reconstruction of Dhamma restore human dignity?"

### 🏛️ Canonical Technical Lexicon
- **Navayāna (The New Vehicle)**: Rationalist, humanist Buddhism oriented toward social justice and liberation from caste oppression.
- **Graded Inequality (विभाजित असमानता)**: An ascending scale of reverence and descending scale of contempt, preventing collective revolt.
- **Dhamma vs. Dharma**: Dhamma is social morality and universal compassion (karuṇā/maitrī); Brahmanical Dharma is ritual law.
- **Constitutional Morality**: Paramount devotion to democratic procedure, secular equality, and legal rights over sectarian dogma.

### ⚔️ The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
**Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):** Orthodox defenders claimed caste was divinely ordained by Puruṣa Sūkta (Ṛgveda 10.90) and justified by retributive past karma.

**Siddhānta (Defense):** Ambedkar in Annihilation of Caste and The Buddha and His Dhamma argued: 'Caste is not merely a division of labour; it is a division of labourers.' Any religion that denies human equality is an engine of oppression. True religion is morality that fosters fraternity among living human beings on this earth.

### 📜 Primary Textual Locus
- **Author:** Dr. B. R. Ambedkar
- **Work:** *The Buddha and His Dhamma (1957) & Annihilation of Caste (1936)*
- **Passage:** The Buddha and His Dhamma, Book II, Part II ('Dhamma is Morality'); Annihilation of Caste §§ 14–20

### ⚠️ Central University Interview & UGC NET Trap
> What was Ambedkar's critique of the Buddhist Four Noble Truths? Ambedkar rejected the orthodox interpretation of 'Sarvaṃ Duḥkham' as pessimistic metaphysical inevitability; he insisted socio-economic, human-inflicted suffering must be distinguished from existential sorrow, and the Sangha must model equality.

### 💡 Wisdom Gist
> "Philosophy cannot remain detached in ivory towers while human beings are humiliated. The true test of any philosophical vision is its power to dismantle cruelty and cultivate fraternity."
