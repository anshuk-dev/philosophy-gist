## Swami Vivekananda: Practical Vedānta & Universal Religion (Indian Philosophy)
**School:** Contemporary Indian | **Unit:** UNIT_05

### 🔗 Conceptual Chain (Dialectical Lineage)
- **1. Origin / Prior Knot:** Medieval World-Renouncing Monasticism
- **2. Current Core Pivot:** **Advaitic oneness must not remain in mountain caves; it must be applied practically to uplift humanity through service to Daridra Nārāyaṇa.**
- **3. Next Horizon:** Sri Aurobindo's Integral Yoga & Modern Engaged Vedānta

### 🎯 The Fundamental Dilemma
> "How can the supreme philosophical truth of Advaita (oneness of Brahman) be translated into social action, moral courage, and global religious harmony?"

### 🏛️ Canonical Technical Lexicon
- **Practical Vedānta**: The application of non-dual spiritual realization to everyday social, educational, and ethical life.
- **Daridra Nārāyaṇa (God in the Poor)**: Serving the suffering and downtrodden as direct worship of the living Divine.
- **Universal Religion**: Harmony of all faiths as different paths ascending the same spiritual mountain, avoiding dogmatism.
- **Four Yogas (Jñāna, Bhakti, Karma, Rāja)**: A balanced, scientific synthesis of intellect, devotion, action, and psychological meditation.

### ⚔️ The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
**Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):** Critics claimed Vedānta fosters quietism, fatalism, and social passivity because it treats the world as an illusion.

**Siddhānta (Defense):** Vivekananda roared at the Parliament of Religions (1893) and in his London lectures: 'If you cannot worship your brother man, the manifested God, how can you worship an unmanifested God?' If all souls are Brahman, all human beings are inherently divine and equal. Service is not condescending charity; it is self-worship.

### 📜 Primary Textual Locus
- **Author:** Swami Vivekananda
- **Work:** *Complete Works of Swami Vivekananda*
- **Passage:** Volume II: 'Practical Vedānta' (Lectures delivered in London, November 1896)

### ⚠️ Central University Interview & UGC NET Trap
> Central University boards frequently ask: 'How does Vivekananda's concept of Maya differ from Shankara's?' Vivekananda did NOT treat Maya as an ontological illusion or theory; he called Maya 'a simple statement of fact of what we are and what we see around us'—the inescapable contradiction between the ideal and the real.

### 💡 Wisdom Gist
> "Strength is life; weakness is death. True spirituality is not running away from the world, but bringing the light of the divine into the dark corners of human suffering."
