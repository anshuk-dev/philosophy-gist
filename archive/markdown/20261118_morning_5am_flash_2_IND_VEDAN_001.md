## Adhyāsa, Māyā & The Two Tiers of Reality (Indian Philosophy)
**School:** Advaita Vedānta | **Unit:** UNIT_01

### 🔗 Conceptual Chain (Dialectical Lineage)
- **1. Origin / Prior Knot:** Vivartavāda (The effect as an appearance of the cause)
- **2. Current Core Pivot:** **The perceived diversity of the universe is a superimposition (Adhyāsa) upon pure non-dual consciousness (Brahman) via inexplicable ignorance (Māyā/Avidyā).**
- **3. Next Horizon:** Rāmānuja's Critique of Advaita (The Saptavidhā Anupapatti)

### 🎯 The Fundamental Dilemma
> "How can the pure, undivided, immutable Brahman appear as a cosmos of suffering, multiplicity, and individual bound souls (jīvas)?"

### 🏛️ Canonical Technical Lexicon
- **Adhyāsa (अध्यास)**: Superimposition: the apparent presentation of an attribute of one thing onto another (smṛtirūpaḥ paratra pūrvadṛṣṭāvabhāsaḥ).
- **Māyā / Avidyā (माया / अविद्या)**: The cosmic principle of illusion possessing two powers: Āvaraṇa (veiling reality) and Vikṣepa (projecting multiplicity).
- **Sattā-traividhya (सत्ता-त्रैविध्य)**: Three tiers of reality: Pāramārthika (Absolute/Brahman), Vyāvahārika (Empirical/World), Prātibhāsika (Illusory/Dreams).
- **Sākṣin (साक्षी)**: The witness-consciousness that illuminates both knowledge and ignorance without participating in action.

### ⚔️ The Dialectic: Objection & Resolution
**Pūrvapakṣa (Opponent):** The realist asks: The subject (knower) and object (known) are as opposed as light and darkness. How can the attributes of the insentient world be superimposed upon the pure conscious self?

**Siddhānta (Defense):** Śaṅkara opens the Brahmasūtra-Bhāṣya by declaring Adhyāsa natural and beginningless (naisargiko 'yaṃ lokavyavahāraḥ). Just as a blameless sky appears blue or contaminated by dust, consciousness misidentifies with body and mind due to innate nescience.

### 📜 Primary Textual Locus
- **Author:** Śaṅkarācārya
- **Work:** *Brahmasūtra-Bhāṣya*
- **Passage:** Adhyāsa-Bhāṣya (Preamble to BS 1.1.1)

### ⚠️ Central University Interview & UGC NET Trap
> Did Śaṅkara claim the world is a complete hallucination like a dream? NO! That is the trap. Śaṅkara vigorously refutes Buddhist subjective idealism (Yogācāra) in BS 2.2.28; the empirical world possesses Vyāvahārika Sattā (objective reality for all practical purposes) until sublated by Brahman-knowledge.

### 💡 Wisdom Gist
> "Much of our anxiety and suffering stems from superimposing ephemeral psychological identities onto the silent, untroubled witness within."
